TITLE: How to learn Dynamic Programming without hating it
SOURCE_TYPE: transcript

So today I want to talk about dynamic programming, because every week someone in my DMs says DP is impossible. It's not impossible. You're just learning it in the wrong order.

Most students open a DP sheet, see a tabulation solution with a 2D array, and try to memorise it. That is the mistake. You cannot memorise your way through DP because every problem has a slightly different state.

Step one is recursion. Write the brute force recursive solution first. Ask yourself: what is the smallest version of this problem, and how does the bigger answer depend on smaller answers? If you can't write the recursion, you don't understand the problem yet.

Step two is memoization. Notice that your recursion is solving the same subproblem again and again. Add a cache. That's it. Fibonacci goes from exponential to linear just by storing results.

Step three is tabulation. Now flip the recursion upside down and fill a table from the base case. This is where the 2D arrays come from, and now they make sense because you derived them.

Step four, optional, is space optimisation. Often you only need the previous row, so you can go from O(n*m) space to O(m).

In my last batch, students who followed recursion, then memoization, then tabulation solved roughly twice as many DP problems in a month compared to students who jumped straight to tabulation. Start with ten classic problems: climbing stairs, house robber, coin change, longest common subsequence, 0/1 knapsack, and so on. Don't do two hundred random problems. Do ten deeply.
