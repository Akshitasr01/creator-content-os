TITLE: Why your first investment should be boring
SOURCE_TYPE: article

When people get their first salary, the first question is usually which stock or which crypto to buy. I think that's the wrong first question. The first question is: what happens if your income stops for six months?

An emergency fund is money you keep aside for exactly that situation: job loss, a medical bill, a family emergency. The common guideline is three to six months of essential expenses. Not your salary, your essential expenses: rent, food, EMIs, insurance premiums, utilities.

Where should it live? Somewhere boring, safe and quick to access. A savings account or a liquid fund works. It should not be in equity, because the moment you need it might be the moment markets are down 30%.

Build it gradually. If your essential expenses are 25,000 a month, a three-month fund is 75,000. Saving 10,000 a month gets you there in under eight months. Automate the transfer on salary day so you never see the money.

Only after the emergency fund and basic health and term insurance are in place should you start long-term investing. This order feels slow, but it's what stops you from selling investments at the worst possible time.
